Audio Routing with Voicemeeter

Make teammates hear your soundboard — and let them trigger it.

💬 Stuck? Ask on the Discord

Why you need this

Out of the box, TriggerWord plays sounds to your speakers. For the real fun — teammates in Discord or game chat hearing your drops, or their voices triggering sounds — the audio has to be mixed and routed, and Windows can't do that alone. Voicemeeter Banana (free) is the standard tool. Install it, reboot (really), and come back.

The 60-second mental model

Voicemeeter is a mixing desk between your apps and your hardware:

  • Strips (left side) are inputs: your real mic, plus two virtual ones — Voicemeeter Input (VAIO) and Voicemeeter Aux Input (AUX) — that Windows apps can play into like fake speakers.
  • Buses (the A/B buttons on every strip) are outputs: A1 = your actual headphones. B1 / B2 = virtual microphones that other apps can listen to.
  • Routing is just: which strips light up which A/B buttons. That's the whole trick.

Recipe 1 — Teammates hear your soundboard

  1. In Voicemeeter, set Hardware Input 1 to your real microphone, and A1 (top right) to your headphones.
  2. On the mic strip: turn on B1 (and A1 if you monitor yourself).
  3. In TriggerWord, set the output device to Voicemeeter Input (VB-Audio Voicemeeter VAIO).
  4. On the Voicemeeter VAIO strip: turn on B1, and A1 so you hear your own sounds.
  5. In Discord (or the game), set the input device / microphone to Voicemeeter Out B1.

Now B1 carries your voice + the soundboard mixed together, and Discord thinks that mix is your microphone. Done.

Discord tip: turn OFF noise suppression (Krisp) and echo cancellation for best results — they treat music and sound effects as "noise" and will eat your drops.

Recipe 2 — Teammates trigger sounds

Point TriggerWord's ears at the people you're talking to:

  1. In Discord, set the output device to Voicemeeter Aux Input (VB-Audio Voicemeeter AUX VAIO) — teammate voices now arrive on the AUX strip.
  2. On the AUX strip: turn on A1 (so you still hear them) and B2.
  3. Point Chrome at Voicemeeter Out B2 — not just TriggerWord. Open http://localhost:8002 in a normal browser tab, click the 🎤 icon in the address bar, and choose it from the dropdown. Chrome decides what speech recognition listens to, and it remembers this per address.
  4. Want your own voice to keep triggering too? Turn on B2 on your mic strip as well — now B2 carries you and your teammates.
⚠️ The one rule: never route the soundboard into B2. The VAIO strip (where TriggerWord plays) must have B2 off — otherwise the soundboard hears itself and a clip that says a trigger word re-triggers forever. TriggerWord pauses its own listening during playback, but don't tempt it.

The whole picture

If it sounds wrong

  • No sound at all: A1 (top-right in Voicemeeter) isn't set to your headphones, or Voicemeeter started after your apps. Start Voicemeeter first, then restart Discord/TriggerWord.
  • Robotic / crackling audio: sample-rate mismatch. Set your mic, headphones, and all Voicemeeter devices to 48000 Hz in Windows Sound settings, and in Voicemeeter's Menu → System Settings.
  • Teammates hear themselves (echo): the AUX strip (Discord voices) has B1 on. Discord audio must go to your ears (A1) and the trigger listener (B2) — never back out through B1.
  • Sounds trigger themselves: the VAIO strip has B2 on. Turn it off — see the rule above.
  • Everything broke after a Windows update: Windows loves to reset default devices. Check Windows Sound settings first, then Voicemeeter's A1.
Got Voicemeeter Potato? Same idea, more room: extra strips and buses (B3, more hardware outs) let you keep separate buses for "what TriggerWord hears" and "what goes to my mic" — worth it if you also juggle game audio, music, and streaming.
