Getting started

  1. Download the repository — green Code button → Download ZIP → unzip it somewhere you'll find again.
  2. Double-click start-triggerword.bat. TriggerWord opens in its own window.
  3. Click "Allow" when it asks for your microphone. Nothing works without this — if you deny it, every device dropdown will look empty (see Troubleshooting).
  4. Import a soundpack (below) or add sounds one at a time.
  5. Click Listen for triggers and say one of your trigger words.
You need Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge. Speech recognition and per-sound output routing are Chromium features — Firefox and Safari won't work. You also need Python ("Add Python to PATH" ticked during install). No Python packages are required.
The fine line. A sound that lands once is a joke; the same sound five times a minute is a reason to mute you. Pick trigger words you say at moments that actually deserve a sound. Cooldowns exist on purpose — leave them on. The people listening did not consent to an airhorn.

Updating

Double-click update-triggerword.bat in the app folder. It closes TriggerWord, downloads the latest version, replaces the app files and starts it back up. Your sounds, triggers and settings are never touched — they live in your browser's storage, not in the app folder.

TriggerWord also checks for new releases about once a day and shows a small notice when one is out. Dismissing it hides that version for good.

Adding your own sounds

  • Add a trigger with the ➕ button, give it a word (or several, comma-separated — they all fire the same sounds).
  • Attach one or many sounds — with several, one is picked at random each time. Variety is what keeps a trigger funny.
  • Cooldown is per-trigger, in seconds. Short for stingers, long for songs.
  • Quick Record (Ctrl+R by default) records from your mic straight into a sound — great for making your own drops on the spot.
  • Time-Shift keeps a rolling buffer of the last seconds of audio, so when something funny happens you can capture it after it happened.

Favorites & keyboard shortcuts

  • ❤️ a trigger to make it a favorite. Favorites fire from F1–F12, Alt+1–9 and the numpad, in the order they appear.
  • Assign any key to any trigger in Settings → Trigger Shortcuts (press-to-learn).
  • Control shortcuts bind keys to app functions: start/stop listening, quick record, time-shift capture, and Fade Out All Sounds.
  • By default a pressed shortcut bypasses the cooldown — a deliberate press should always fire. Turn that off in Settings → Trigger Shortcuts if you want presses to respect cooldowns like voice does.
  • Pressing a trigger while another sound is playing chokes the playing one (cuts it instantly, drum-pad style) instead of layering both.

Stopping sounds — the "quiet" word

Three ways to stop everything that's playing, all with a smooth 1-second fade instead of a hard cut:

  • Say "quiet" — this works even while a song is blasting; it's the only word TriggerWord listens for during playback.
  • Click the 🔇 button in the header.
  • Bind a key to Fade Out All Sounds in control shortcuts.

This is what makes full songs usable on a soundboard: trigger a track, talk over it, and kill it on demand.

Levelling — why nothing blasts your listeners

Sounds collected from everywhere arrive at wildly different volumes. TriggerWord measures every sound once when you add it and turns the loud ones down so they sit together. If one still isn't right, every sound has a trim slider (±12 dB) and an auto button to reset it. Because levelling only ever turns sounds down, everything ends up a touch quieter overall — compensate with the master volume.

Backups — read this once

Your sounds live in your browser's storage. Browsers get cleared and profiles get reset. Export backup (everything) writes one ZIP with your sounds, triggers, favorites, shortcuts, settings and volume trims — keep it somewhere that isn't your browser. Import backup puts it all back. A backup ZIP is also a normal soundpack, so it can be shared with friends.

Look & feel

  • 🌙 Dark theme — toggle in the header, remembered between sessions.
  • Install as an app — open http://localhost:8002 in a normal Chrome tab and click the install icon in the address bar. You get a real TriggerWord app window with its own icon and yellow title bar.
  • The yellow border is on purpose. It means this window is TriggerWord.

Advanced: full mode & global hotkeys

Out of the box TriggerWord runs on a zero-dependency file server. If you install the optional packages (pip install -r requirements.txt — a large download including torch), the same start-triggerword.bat automatically switches to full mode:

  • A local Whisper transcription server (offline speech recognition).
  • Global hotkeys: keys F13–F24 (macro pads, stream decks, BLE remotes) fire triggers even when the TriggerWord window isn't focused.
  • Everything runs hidden — no terminal windows. Closing the TriggerWord window shuts the whole thing down after a few seconds.

Troubleshooting

The device dropdowns are empty

Microphone permission was denied at some point and the browser remembered it. Open chrome://settings/content/microphone, add http://localhost:8002 under Allowed, and restart the app.

Nothing happens when I speak

Check the microphone dropdown is set to the mic you're actually using and that the page says it's listening. Speech recognition needs a moment of clear speech — it won't catch one muttered syllable.

Sounds play to the wrong device

Set the output device in the app, not just in Windows. Routing to a virtual cable is how you get soundboard audio into a stream.

I lost my soundpack

Import your backup ZIP. If you don't have one, make one now — seriously.
