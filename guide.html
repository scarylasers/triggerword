Get started in four steps

  1. Download the installer — TriggerWord-Setup.exe from the latest release.
  2. Run it. It installs just for you (no admin needed) and makes a desktop shortcut. Everything is included — you don't need to install Python or anything else.
  3. Open TriggerWord and click Allow when it asks for your microphone. Nothing works without this.
  4. Click Listen for triggers and say one of the words on the cards.
"Windows protected your PC"? That blue box appears for any installer without an expensive code-signing certificate — it is not a virus warning. Click More info → Run anyway. You can always read every line of the source on GitHub.
You also need Chrome or Edge installed — the speech recognition and per-sound audio routing are Chromium features. No account and no sign-in; your sounds and settings stay on this machine.

Worth knowing: the listening is done by Chrome, not by TriggerWord, and unless Chrome has on-device speech models installed it sends that microphone audio to Google to turn into text — the same as any site using this feature. TriggerWord never receives or stores it, and your sounds are never uploaded by anything.

Prefer to run from source? Grab the repo, install Python, and use start-triggerword.bat — that route also unlocks the advanced features below.

🐰 Please use this tool responsibly. There is a fine line between funny and annoying, and a listening soundboard sits right on it. A sound that lands once is a joke; the same sound five times a minute is a reason to mute you. Pick words you say at moments that deserve a sound, and leave cooldowns on — they exist on purpose. The people listening did not consent to an airhorn.

The starter pack

⚙️ Settings → Import backup → pick TriggerWord-Starter-Pack.zip from the app folder.

Some samples and a few full songs to get you started — say the word, get the sound. Open a card to see the words it answers to, and change any of them to whatever you actually say.

One word works everywhere, whatever is loaded: say "quiet" and everything playing fades out, even mid-song.

Your own sounds

  • ➕ Add a trigger — give it a word (or several, comma-separated — any of them fires it) and attach a sound.
  • Group sounds — add more sounds to the same trigger and one plays at random each time. A trigger with 10 variations stays funny way longer than one sound on repeat.
  • 🔧 menu imports and exports whole soundpacks as ZIPs — share them with friends.
  • ❤️ Favorite a trigger and it fires from F1–F12. Any key can be assigned to any trigger in ⚙️ Settings.
  • Cooldowns are per-trigger — short for stingers, long for songs. They're what keeps a soundboard funny instead of annoying.
  • Tabs — organize your board into pages (➕ in the tab bar makes one; the ✏️ edit button on a card moves it there). All and ⭐ Favorites are always present.

Stopping sounds — "quiet"

Say "quiet", click 🔇, or bind a key to Fade Out All Sounds — everything fades out in a second, even while a full song is playing. Pressing a new trigger mid-sound cuts the old one off (no layering). That's what makes full songs usable live.

Settings & backups

Export backup (everything) saves your whole setup — sounds, triggers, favorites, shortcuts, trims — to one ZIP. Your library lives in your browser's storage, so make a backup and keep it somewhere safe.

Updating

Installed with the installer? Download the new TriggerWord-Setup.exe and run it — it installs over the top. Close TriggerWord first or it will ask you to.

Running from source? Run update-triggerword.bat in the app folder.

Either way your sounds and settings are never touched — they live in your browser, not the app folder. The app also tells you when a new version is out.

Advanced — the deep stuff

  • Quick Record (Ctrl+R) — records your mic straight into a sound. Make a drop on the spot, mid-stream. Ctrl+1 plays your latest recording.
  • Time-Shift (Ctrl+T) — keeps a rolling buffer of the last seconds of audio. Something funny just happened? Ctrl+R captures it after the fact. Buffer length is in ⚙️ Settings.
  • Control shortcuts — bind any key to app functions: start/stop listening, quick record, time-shift capture, fade out all sounds.
  • Cooldown bypass — by default a pressed shortcut fires immediately, even mid-cooldown, and chokes whatever was playing. Voice triggers always respect cooldowns. Toggle in ⚙️ Settings → Trigger Shortcuts.
  • Levelling & trim — every sound is measured once and loud ones are turned down so nothing blasts anyone. Each sound has a ±12 dB trim slider and an auto-reset.
  • Output routing — pick which output device sounds play to, per your setup. Want teammates to hear your soundboard, or even trigger it with their own voices? That takes Voicemeeter — follow the Audio Routing Guide.
  • Full mode — pip install -r requirements.txt (big download: torch + Whisper) and the same start-triggerword.bat upgrades itself: offline Whisper speech recognition, global hotkeys F13–F24 that fire triggers with the window unfocused (macro pads, stream decks, BLE remotes), everything running hidden, and closing the TriggerWord window shuts the whole thing down.

If something's wrong

  • Device dropdowns empty? Mic permission was denied. Open chrome://settings/content/microphone, allow http://localhost:8002, restart.
  • Not hearing you — and you have more than one microphone? This is the big one. Chrome picks the microphone for speech recognition, not TriggerWord. The dropdown in the app sets the level meter and recording; Chrome's own choice decides what gets transcribed, and the two can disagree.
    To set Chrome's choice: open http://localhost:8002 in a normal browser tab, click the 🎤 microphone icon in the address bar, and pick your microphone from the dropdown there. It sticks for that address, so the app window uses it from then on. The app window has no address bar, which is why you have to do it in a tab once.
  • Sounds on the wrong output? Set the output device in the app, not just Windows.
  • Anything else — ask on the Discord.
